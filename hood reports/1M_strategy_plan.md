## 1. The Math — What This Actually Requires

Going from $73,000 to $1,000,000 in ten years is hard. It is not unprecedented and it is not impossible, but anyone who tells you $1M is a base-case outcome is selling you a story. Here are the four realistic scenarios, with the option-income engine treated as a separate contribution stream because that is what it functionally is.

| Scenario | Equity-book CAGR | Option income/yr | Roth contribution/yr | Terminal value (2036) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear case | 12% | $5,000 | $7,725 | ~$520,000 |
| Realistic base | 18% | $12,000 | $7,725 | ~$745,000 |
| Stretch | 22% | $18,000 | $7,725 | ~$1,020,000 |
| Bull | 25% | $25,000 | $7,725 | ~$1,350,000 |

The honest framing: **$700,000 to $850,000 is the realistic base case**. $1M is achievable only if both the equity book hits roughly 22% CAGR (double the long-run S&P average) AND the option-income engine inflects from breakeven to net positive $15-20k/year. Right now the option engine is essentially scratch over the trailing two years, so the lift required is on both axes simultaneously.

That is fine. Call $1M the stretch goal, $750k the base case, and recognize that $750k by age 38-ish for a software developer in Tallahassee is a genuinely strong outcome. The strategy below is built to maximize the probability of the base case while preserving the upside path to $1M if the option engine matures.

## 2. Three-Account Specialization

The single biggest strategic shift is dividing the three accounts by job. Right now each account does a bit of everything — that produces wash-sale problems, tax-friction on what should be tax-advantaged trades, and cognitive overhead. Going forward each account has a single role.

**Roth IRA — $41,000 — The Income Engine + Compounder**

This is where the credit-spread cycling lives, full stop. No wash-sale rules apply, assignments are tax-free events, and any gains compound tax-free for life. It is also where the highest-volatility growth names should sit — NVDA, TTD, ZETA, RDW, WULF, CIFR — because their explosive upside is fully captured tax-free. Long-term core compounders like a chunk of GOOGL and a small SCHG position can also live here as the stable foundation that the spread engine cycles around.

**SoFi Individual — $11,977 — Buy-and-Hold Quality Equity**

This account is too small to run options on efficiently, and its track record at active trading is negative ($254 realized loss in 2025 plus $1,250 of disallowed wash-sale losses). It becomes the simple, clean buy-and-hold sleeve. Year-end 2026 we harvest the losers (BMNU, SMPL, PATH) and redeploy into a small basket of quality compounders — GOOGL, SCHG ETF anchor, maybe a healthcare name. No options. No fast trading. The mandate is "five names, hold for ten years."

**Robinhood Individual — $20,000 net liq with $12,500 margin debit — Wind Down to Lean**

This is the problem account. Eighty-three percent options allocation, sixty-three percent margin leverage, and eighty distinct option spread legs is more cognitive load than upside. The plan is to gradually wind down options activity here, harvest realized profits to extinguish the margin debit over eighteen to twenty-four months, and hold a small basket of high-conviction equity (AMKR, SLB, NVO, VG, GOOG, NVDA) plus the existing LEAP debit spreads to expiration. New credit spreads should not be opened in this account — route those to the Roth.

## 8. Option Strategy Preservation Rules

The credit-spread engine is working — $7,140 net premium collected in Q1 2026 across both accounts is real. Don't break it. But discipline it.

**Rule 1 — Roth only for new credit spreads.** Wash sale + assignment tax events make the Individual account a worse home for cycling. Going forward, all new put credit spreads and call credit spreads open in the Roth.

**Rule 2 — Hold losers to expiration.** Your high-credit strategy (~85% of width) means losers should be held to expiration where they cap at the actual max loss. Pre-expiration close at -1.5x credit is the worst possible outcome. The IREN, HIMS, and CRWV positions are in this state right now — let them run to expiration.

**Rule 3 — Cap total premium at risk to 15% of book.** With a $73k book that's ~$11,000 of premium-at-risk across all open spreads. You're currently well above that on the Individual side. Wind down to compliance over the next twelve months by letting expirations roll off and not replacing one-for-one.

**Rule 4 — No more leveraged single-name ETFs.** BMNU and TSLZ are object lessons. Daily-reset 2× and 3× ETFs decay even when the underlying is flat. Never again.

**Rule 5 — SBET stays as-is.** Lottery ticket sized correctly at $531 max risk, exit at $0.06 (double) or TLH by Dec 20. Don't add to it.

## 10. Reality Check — What Has to Go Right

For $1M to actually happen by April 2036, all of these need to be roughly true:

1. The equity book averages 18-22% CAGR. That's better than the long-run S&P average of 10% but well below NVDA's recent run-rate. It's possible if the AI capex cycle continues and your tech-tilted book benefits.
2. The option-income engine inflects from breakeven (lifetime -$760 across taxable accounts) to net positive $12-18k/year sustainably. The Q1 2026 inflection is encouraging but it's one quarter.
3. No major drawdown timed badly. A 40% bear market in years 8-10 of the plan is very damaging because there's not enough time to recover.
4. You actually max the Roth every year. Missing one year of $7,725 contributions costs roughly $35,000 of terminal value at 22% CAGR.
5. Wash-sale and tax friction stay contained. The 2025 $1,250 of disallowed losses is a warning shot — this needs to stop, which the three-account specialization fixes.

For $750-800k (the realistic base case) only items 1, 4, and 5 need to be roughly true, which is far more achievable. Optimize for the base case, build in optionality for the stretch.
